This page explains how restoration works, describes every service in detail, warns about common mistakes, and lists the areas we serve.

Restore or Rebuild?

Restoration is usually the better fit when the pool's structure is sound and the problems are cosmetic or mechanical:

  • The plaster feels rough or shows stains and discoloration
  • The tile line and coping look dated or damaged
  • The deck is cracked or faded
  • The equipment is old and costs more to run than it should
  • The shape or layout no longer suits how you use the space

If you are not sure which category your pool falls into, start with a professional inspection. It gives you a written picture of the pool before you spend money on the wrong project.

Planning a Remodeling Project

  1. Inspect first. Book a professional inspection so you know what is worn and what is not.
  2. Fix problems before finishes. Leaks and equipment faults should be handled before you invest in a new surface.
  3. Choose your scope. Many owners combine resurfacing, tile and coping, and deck renewal into one project.
  4. Add the finishing touches. Consider water features, lighting, or equipment upgrades while the pool is already being worked on.
  5. Protect the result. Regular upkeep keeps a renewed pool looking new.

Common Remodeling Mistakes to Avoid

  • Finishing a surface over an active problem. A new finish on top of an unfixed leak or crack can fail early. Repair first, then resurface.
  • Doing the work in the wrong order. Equipment, plumbing, and structural work come before visible finishes, so nothing gets torn up twice.
  • Treating tile, coping, and deck as separate jobs. They meet at the pool edge, so planning them together gives a more consistent result and can reduce disruption.
  • Ignoring the equipment. A beautiful pool with an outdated pump still costs more to run. Ask whether upgrades make sense while the pool is being worked on.
  • Skipping the written scope. Get the work, materials, and equipment described in writing before you approve a quote.
  • Forgetting upkeep. A renewed pool still needs regular cleaning and water balancing to stay that way.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know whether my pool can be restored?

If the shell is structurally sound and the issues are surface, tile, deck, or equipment related, restoration is usually possible. An inspection confirms this.

Can I do just one part of a remodel?

Yes. Resurfacing, tile and coping, deck renewal, lighting, and equipment can each be done on their own or combined.
